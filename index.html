Faculty Title Guide

Choosing Faculty Titles

Start the title check

A clear, practical framework for matching a faculty appointment to the level of work, evidence, and institutional expectations.

  • Match title to rank, role, and responsibilities.
  • Use the faculty handbook as the final authority.
  • Document evidence before final approval.

Core rule

Faculty titles should reflect the work performed, the level of independent scholarship, and the expected contribution to teaching, research, and service.

Quick guide

Typical faculty title patterns

Early-career

Assistant Professor

Common for faculty who are establishing a scholarly record and are expected to grow into independent contributors.

Mid-career

Associate Professor

Usually signals sustained contribution, evidence of impact, and readiness for broader institutional leadership.

Senior

Professor

Signals national or international recognition, sustained professional excellence, and leadership in the discipline.

Decision lens

What to evaluate before choosing a title

Primary considerations

  • Teaching responsibility and curriculum leadership
  • Research productivity and independence
  • Service, mentorship, and governance work
  • Faculty handbook and promotion standards
  • Institutional peer comparators

Common red flags

  • Using a title that exceeds the role without evidence
  • Ignoring discipline-specific standards
  • Matching the title to title alone rather than portfolio
  • Overlooking required approvals and policy review

Examples

Typical scenarios

New hire with doctoral training

Often begins as Assistant Professor when the role emphasizes creating a research trajectory and building teaching effectiveness.

Promoted faculty with sustained impact

Associate Professor commonly fits a faculty member with a consistent record of scholarship, teaching excellence, and service contributions.

Distinguished scholar and leader

Professor is typically reserved for individuals with sustained national recognition, leadership, and enduring institutional contribution.
